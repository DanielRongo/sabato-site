Why this category phones

Twelve weeks of demand, and a phone line staffed for fifty-two.

Garden retail concentrates most of its year into a short season, and the calls concentrate harder still — the first warm Saturday produces a volume no permanently staffed line is sized for, and abandoned calls in a peak skew towards buyers who were ready to spend.

The rest of the year the same line handles spares: a part number off a machine bought four seasons ago, which is a lookup, not a conversation.

Five questions a garden line hears every day — and what has to be true in your systems to answer them

Two very different jobs share one phone line here: seasonal sizing calls from new buyers, and spares lookups from owners. The data behind them is different, and both are answerable.

“What size mower for eight hundred square metres?”

Cutting width and recommended plot range on the product, matched to the area and terrain the caller describes.

attr.cutting_width_cm · attr.plot_max_m2

“When is it back in stock?”

Inbound purchase order date checked against how much season is left — a restock landing in September is a different answer from one landing in May.

po.expected_at · season.cutoff

“I need the blade for a model I bought in 2021.”

Spare-part compatibility by machine model and year, including superseded part numbers.

spares.by_model(year) · part.superseded_by[]

“Will it arrive before the weekend?”

Carrier cut-off against the caller’s postcode — the question that decides the order in a category driven by two dry days.

carrier.cutoff · stock.by_wh

“Does it need servicing, and do you do it?”

Service interval on the product and whether you offer it, so the answer is a booking rather than a maybe.

attr.service_interval · service.available

The data reality behind a garden answer

The seasonal shape is the whole problem here. Concurrency matters more than in any other category on this list: the agent takes every call at once rather than queueing them, which is exactly what a peak Saturday needs.

THE HONEST PART

Bad source data produces confident wrong answers. If a field is stale, the agent will speak it fluently — and a wrong answer at speed is worse than a slow right one, because it converts into an order that fails. We audit the fields this page depends on before the pilot, and we cap the agent to hand over on anything unmapped.

Sizing attributes

Cutting width, plot range, power — as fields, so a plot size maps to a model.

Inbound purchase orders

Expected dates by variant, so a restock promise is a date rather than a shrug.

Spares compatibility

By machine model and year, with superseded part numbers followed.

Carrier cut-offs by postcode

In a weather-driven category, “before the weekend” is the buying decision.

Service availability

Intervals and whether you service, so the answer is a booking.

Common questions

What happens when the season peaks?

Concurrency is the point: the agent answers every call simultaneously rather than queueing them. Peak Saturdays are when abandoned calls spike, and abandoned calls in this category skew towards buyers who were ready to spend.

Can it handle spare-part lookups for old machines?

Where you hold spares compatibility by model and year, yes — including following superseded part numbers. That is the off-season half of this line’s work and it is almost entirely lookup.

Is it worth it if we are only busy three months a year?

That is the honest question for this category. A voice layer has no seasonal hiring lead time and no training cost, which is what makes a short season workable — but if your peak is small, we will say so on the intro call rather than sell you one.

Can it call people back when stock lands?

Yes, and in garden retail it is worth more than an email, because season demand is time-boxed. The agent rings, confirms, and takes the order on the same call.

What does it need to go live?

Products with sizing attributes, inbound purchase order dates, spares compatibility and your carrier cut-offs. The spares data is usually the one that needs work.
