Why this category phones

A wrong part is a return, a refit and a customer who buys the next one somewhere else.

Parts buyers are not browsing. They have a car in pieces, or a customer waiting, and a specific question: does this fit. Get it wrong and you pay the return, the courier, and often a labour claim from a garage that already started the job.

The product page can host a registration lookup, and it should. It still cannot answer “mine’s a facelift model, does that change the caliper?” - which is exactly the call that arrives instead.

Fitment data ages badly. Manufacturers revise, supersede and re-reference constantly, and a table that was correct two years ago will quietly produce wrong answers today. We audit these before the pilot.

Common questions

Can a voice agent really confirm fitment on the phone?

Only as well as your fitment table allows. Given a registration or VIN it decodes the variant and matches the part family, including build-date ranges. Where the table has no record for that variant it says so and hands over - it does not infer fitment from a similar model.

What about trade customers who call every day?

They are the ones who benefit most, because the call is short and repetitive: part, vehicle, stock, cut-off. The agent recognises the account from the number and skips the parts a regular caller does not need to hear.

Can it handle a caller who does not know their VIN?

Yes - it asks for the registration first, and falls back to make, model, year and engine, flagging that the answer is less certain than a VIN match. That caveat is spoken, not buried.

Does it work out of hours and at weekends?

Yes, and in this category that matters more than most: a lot of parts buying happens on a Saturday with the car already apart.

What does it need from our store to go live?

Read access to products and orders, your fitment and supersession tables, and stock by warehouse with cut-off times. If fitment lives in a supplier feed rather than your catalogue, we read the feed.
