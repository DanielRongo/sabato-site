Why this category phones

A sofa is a logistics decision that happens to look like a purchase.

The buyer has decided on the sofa. What stops the order is the doorway, the lift, the Tuesday they are actually at home, and whether the old one gets taken away. None of that is on the product page, and all of it is knowable.

Get it wrong and the failure is expensive in a way clothing returns never are: a two-person crew, a wasted slot, and a piece of furniture that comes back to a warehouse as a one-off.

Five questions a furniture line hears every day - and what has to be true in your systems to answer them

This category needs two things most catalogues hold badly: packed dimensions as distinct from assembled dimensions, and a delivery calendar the phone can actually read.

“Will it get up a turning staircase?”

Packed dimensions - not assembled - plus whether the piece separates, which is the fact that decides most access questions.

product.packed_dims · product.modular

“When can you deliver, and can I pick the day?”

Live two-person delivery calendar for the caller’s postcode, booked on the call rather than confirmed by email later.

delivery.slots(postcode)

“Do you take the old sofa away?”

Removal service availability by area and its price, added to the order while the caller is still on the line.

service.removal.by_area

“It says made to order - how long really?”

Production lead time on the specific fabric and frame combination, not the catalogue-wide figure.

variant.production_days

“Can I see the fabric before I commit?”

Swatch availability for that fabric, dispatched on the call - the cheapest thing you can do to prevent a colour return.

swatch.available · swatch.sku

What the call sounds like

Inbound · 2 min 22 s · language: English · outcome: slot booked, removal added

CALLER

I like the three-seater in the grey, but I’ve got a table in front of the sofa and I’m worried the room is too tight.

AGENT

Let’s do it properly. How wide is the wall you’d put it against, and how deep is the room?

CALLER

Four twenty by three sixty, roughly.

AGENT

Then the sofa itself is 205 wide by 95 deep. Against the four-twenty wall that leaves you just over two metres of wall either side, and from the front of the sofa to a standard coffee table you’d have about thirty-eight centimetres to walk past.

CALLER

Is thirty-eight enough?

AGENT

It’s tight but it works - people usually want forty-five or more for a main walkway. If you pull the table forward ten centimetres you’re comfortable. The two-seater in the same range is 165, if you’d rather have the space.

CALLER

No, the three-seater. Does it come in one piece?

AGENT

Two boxes, legs separate, so getting it in is easier than the assembled size suggests. Next delivery on your postcode is Thursday the fourteenth - shall I hold it?

What the agent has to read

The data reality behind a furniture answer

The two fields that matter most in this category are the two most often missing: packed dimensions, and a delivery calendar something other than a human can read.

THE HONEST PART

Bad source data produces confident wrong answers. If a field is stale, the agent will speak it fluently - and a wrong answer at speed is worse than a slow right one, because it converts into an order that fails. We audit the fields this page depends on before the pilot, and we cap the agent to hand over on anything unmapped.

Packed vs assembled dimensions

Both, as separate fields. Access questions are answered by the first and almost never by the second.

Delivery calendar by postcode

Live slots for two-person delivery, bookable on the call.

Removal and assembly services

Availability by area and price, so they can be added while the caller is deciding.

Production lead times

Per fabric and frame combination, not a catalogue-wide average.

Swatch inventory

Which fabrics can be sampled and how fast, because a swatch is cheaper than a returned sofa.

Common questions

Can it really answer whether furniture will fit up a staircase?

It gives the caller the packed dimensions and whether the piece separates, which is what the question actually turns on. It does not survey the house - it hands the buyer the numbers they need and flags when the answer is marginal.

Can it book a delivery slot on the call?

Yes, where your delivery calendar is readable by an API. That is the single highest-value integration in this category, because a slot booked on the call is a slot that does not become three emails.

What about made-to-order lead times?

It reads the production time for the specific fabric and frame the caller is asking about. Catalogue-wide lead times are where furniture merchants generate most of their status calls.

Does it handle the post-delivery problems too?

Damage and assembly issues route to a human with the order context attached. The agent is useful for catching them early, not for resolving a damage claim.

What does it need to go live?

Products with packed dimensions, your delivery calendar, and your service list for removal and assembly. Most furniture merchants have the third in a spreadsheet, which is fine.
