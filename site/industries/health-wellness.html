Why this category phones

A subscription is a relationship with a billing date, and people ring about billing dates.

Wellness brands run on repeat orders, and repeat orders generate a specific, high-volume, low-complexity call: pause it, skip it, move it, change the card, change the address. Every one of those is a database write that someone is currently doing by hand.

The second queue is returns on opened goods, where policy is narrower than in most categories and the answer on the phone has to match the answer in the portal exactly.

Five questions a wellness line hears every day — and what has to be true in your systems to answer them

Four of these are subscription and logistics lookups. The fifth is the one the agent must not answer on its own, and the boundary between them is the most important thing on this page.

“Can I skip next month?”

Subscription state and next charge date, with the skip written back on the call rather than promised by email.

sub.next_charge_at · sub.skip()

“I’m away — can it come a week later, to a different address?”

Delivery date and address on the next scheduled order, updated before the cut-off.

sub.next_order.date · sub.address

“I opened it and it doesn’t agree with me. Can I send it back?”

Your returns policy for opened consumables, which is narrower than most categories and must match the portal exactly.

returns.policy.opened_consumable

“Does this contain any dairy?”

The structured allergen and ingredient list on the product, read verbatim — a list, not an interpretation.

attr.allergens[] · attr.ingredients

“Can I take this with my medication?”

Nothing. This is the hand-off. The agent does not answer health questions, and says so plainly before transferring.

escalate: human · no_advice_policy

The data reality behind a wellness answer

The hard boundary in this category is not technical, it is editorial. The agent reads ingredient lists; it does not interpret them. Anything that sounds like a health question is handed over, and that rule is enforced in the workflow rather than left to judgement.

THE HONEST PART

Bad source data produces confident wrong answers. If a field is stale, the agent will speak it fluently — and a wrong answer at speed is worse than a slow right one, because it converts into an order that fails. We audit the fields this page depends on before the pilot, and we cap the agent to hand over on anything unmapped.

Common questions

Will the agent give health advice?

No. Ingredient and allergen lists are read verbatim from your product data; anything about dosage, interactions, conditions or suitability is handed to a human, and the agent says plainly that it is not going to answer. That boundary is enforced in the workflow, not left to the model’s judgement.

Can it change or cancel a subscription on the call?

Yes, where your subscription platform allows writes — skip, pause, change date, change address. That is the highest-volume request in this category and the one most often handled by hand today.

What about returns on opened consumables?

It checks your actual policy, which in this category is usually narrower than elsewhere. The important part is that the phone answer and the portal answer are the same one.

Is this compliant for a regulated category?

We are not lawyers and will not pretend to be. What we can say is that the agent is constrained to reading your own product data and cannot generate health claims, and every call is transcribed for review. Your regulatory position is yours to confirm.

What does it need to go live?

Subscription platform access, order and product data with structured allergen fields, and your returns policy for opened goods.
