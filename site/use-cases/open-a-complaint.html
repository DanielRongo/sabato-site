THE FIRST SIXTY SECONDS

The complaint isn't the damage. It's the wait.

Something went wrong - the parcel arrived broken, the wrong size shipped, the refund never landed. The customer already knows you made a mistake. What decides whether they stay is what happens in the next minute: a queue and hold music, or somebody taking it down properly. 63% of consumers say they'd switch to a competitor after a single bad experience (Zendesk CX Trends 2025) - and a complaint that goes unanswered for six minutes is two bad experiences, not one. Your team can't always pick up in seconds. Something should.

Switching stat: Zendesk CX Trends 2025.

Heard in seconds, resolved by your team

Escalation rules are yours: anything you flag - an abusive call, a distressed customer, a legal word, a VIP account - goes to a person immediately, mid-call. The agent captures; your team resolves.

What a complaint call sounds like

Example call

Live call - Sabato Agent · 01:34
CALLER

The table arrived today and the top is scratched right across the middle. This was a birthday present.

AGENT

I'm sorry - that shouldn't have happened, and I'll make sure the right team sees this today. Can I take the order number, or the postcode it was delivered to?

CALLER

It's 48120.

AGENT

Thank you. Is the damage on the surface only, or is the packaging damaged too? That tells our logistics team whether it happened in transit.

CALLER

The box was fine. The scratch was already there.

AGENT

Understood - that goes to quality, not the courier. I'm sending you a text now; two photos on that link will let them approve a replacement without a second call. What outcome would you like - replacement or refund?

CALLER

Replacement, if it's quick.

AGENT

Noted as a priority replacement. This is with our furniture team as ticket 48120-R, and someone will call you back before six today. You won't need to explain any of this again.

Store, product and ticket details here are illustrative. The agent never promises a resolution it can't make - it captures, routes, and tells the customer exactly what happens next. Refunds, replacements and goodwill stay with your team.

Tickets your team can act on

Order number, what went wrong, whether the packaging was damaged, what the customer actually wants - captured while it's fresh, in the customer's own words, with photos already requested. Your team opens a ticket that's ready to work instead of a one-line note and a callback.

The handoff nobody dreads

When it does reach a person - and complaints should - they arrive knowing the whole story. The customer doesn't repeat it, the agent doesn't apologise for asking again, and the conversation starts at the resolution instead of the reconstruction.

Start with a pilot, not a promise

We won't tell you an AI can calm an angry customer - that's your team's job, and it always will be. A Sabato pilot measures the two things the agent genuinely changes: how fast a complaint gets answered, and how complete the ticket is when a human opens it. If those don't move, you walk away.
