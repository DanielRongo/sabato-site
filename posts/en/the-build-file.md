A season on running a voice build without being an engineer.

Most writing about voice AI is either a vendor explaining why you need one, or an engineer
explaining how the pieces fit together. Neither is much use to the person who actually has to
decide: whether to start, what to ask for, what it will cost to run, and how to tell whether the
thing in front of them is ready for customers.

That's who this is for. Eleven issues, one subject each, no code.

---

## The issues

**00 · [The twenty calls](/blog/voice-agent-acceptance-test)** — what to test before a voice
agent answers a real customer. *Published.*

**01 · What a voice agent is made of** — the five parts, and the one that isn't a part at all.

**02 · Your prototype works** — and why that tells you less than it feels like it does.

**03 · What it costs to run** — the numbers behind a call, per minute and per month.

**04 · When you should build it yourself** — and the far more common case where you shouldn't.

**05 · Pull 200 calls first** — how to scope a voice agent from what your customers already say.

**06 · The data underneath** — what your ERP has to be able to answer, and what happens when it
can't.

**07 · Europe is not one market** — languages, carriers and expectations, country by country.

**08 · Deflection is not resolution** — three words the industry uses interchangeably and
shouldn't.

**09 · Four numbers** — the metrics worth reporting, and the ones that flatter you.

**10 · Three things we rebuilt** — what we got wrong in production, and what replaced it.
